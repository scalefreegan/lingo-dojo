Mandarin words change meaning with their tone, so this drill is about the
shape of the voice, not about new words. Model each tone as a shape and say
the shape in {{native_language}} as you go: tone 1 is high and flat, held
like a long note; tone 2 rises, like asking "what?" in surprise; tone 3 dips
low (said alone it dips and comes back up a little, but inside a phrase it
mostly stays low, and in Taiwan it usually stays low even alone); tone 4
falls sharply, like a firm "no!"; the light tone is short and soft, with no
shape of its own. Use the five ma syllables (媽, 麻, 馬, 罵, 嗎) as the
first round's words, and give the funny sentence 媽媽罵馬嗎? ("is mom
scolding the horse?") as a reward once they have all five.

In the second round, say one of the ma syllables and have {{learner_name}}
tell you which tone it was (the number or the shape, in
{{native_language}}), then swap: you name a tone and they say it. In the
third round, point out that two third tones in a row turn the first into a
rising one (你好 sounds like ní hǎo). Model the pairs the way
{{target_variant}} speakers say them: speakers from Taiwan often give the
second syllable of 謝謝 a full falling tone rather than a light one, and say
台灣 as Táiwān. Give the romanization only to explain a tone, never instead
of the sound. Success looks like {{learner_name}} hearing which of the four
tones a syllable has most of the time, and saying 你好, 謝謝 and 再見 with
the right shapes.
