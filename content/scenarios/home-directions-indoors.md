The friend gives short directions in {{target_language}} and waits while
{{learner_name}} actually walks, one instruction at a time, then reports
where they are. A target is chosen in the home (a cushion, a book, a mug)
and the learner finds it. Then they swap: the learner directs the friend
around an imagined house, and the friend's replies show whether the
directions worked.

Waiting turns: say one short instruction, then stop and wait quietly. If
the learner walks the wrong way, welcome it and give the right direction
again more simply. If they cannot find the target, hint with "warmer or
colder" or describe where it is. Never rush.

Safety: keep to flat, familiar rooms, with no running, no rushing on
stairs, no climbing, no touching hot, sharp or fragile things, and no
cupboards with chemicals or medicine. Stairs only if they are safe, "at your
own pace". If they walk to the wrong place, describe where they are with them, then give one more direction; after a second miss or an "I can't find it", praise the attempt, reveal where it is and swap roles, never carrying on as if it had been found.
Success looks like {{learner_name}} following at least three
directions and giving at least three of their own, correctly.
