{{learner_name}} walks up to the front desk of a hotel in {{location}} after a day of travel, with a booking in hand. The receptionist is friendly but busy: she or he finds the reservation, asks for identification and payment in whatever way is customary in {{location}}, and explains breakfast, wifi, and checkout. Then, after a short break, {{learner_name}} comes back down because something in the room is wrong: the heating or cooling, hot water, noise, or cleanliness. Let the learner pick the problem, or choose one yourself. The receptionist is sympathetic but practical and offers a fix, a swap, or a delay, speaking natural {{target_variant}} at moderate speed. Do not solve things instantly; make the learner describe the problem clearly. Success looks like confirming the booking, asking about breakfast and checkout, describing the room problem in plain words, and agreeing on a solution politely.
