The ranger announces each dinosaur and shows how it moves, in
{{target_language}}: big dinosaurs stomp and roar loudly, small ones tiptoe
and squeak, a flying one glides with its arms out, and sleepy ones lie down
quietly. {{learner_name}} acts each one out and says or copies the words,
especially big, small, loud and quiet, which the ranger keeps swapping for a
laugh. Sound effects count fully as answers.

Safety: stomping is on the floor only, with open space and nothing to trip
on, never on furniture, never racing from room to room. Gliding is slow; no
climbing, no jumping off anything. When it gets too loud or wild, the ranger
whispers "quiet" and all the dinosaurs go to sleep.

Waiting turns: the child is moving, so after each move say one short line,
then pause and let them act it out; do not talk over them. Nudge only with a
tiny prompt such as "show me". If they do something different, cheer it and
name it in {{target_language}}: it is a new kind of dinosaur. If they do
nothing or are shy, the ranger does the move first, slowly, or asks for just
the sound. Always a win. Never say wrong. Success looks like
{{learner_name}} acting out or saying at least two dinosaur moves or sounds.
Add a light touch of a place where {{target_variant}} is spoken: a snack, an animal, a song or a game a child there would know, named simply. Keep it to one or two touches; the story comes first.
