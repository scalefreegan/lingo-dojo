The keeper's dinosaurs are hungry. {{learner_name}} compares them first (big
or small, tall or short; toy dinosaurs, or just the imagination), then goes
to get food for them: the keeper asks for a fruit or snack, like a banana or
an apple, and says the {{target_language}} word slowly. The child gets it
together with a grown-up, from the table or the fruit bowl, not alone from
the fridge or cupboards. They name what they bring, say how many, and show it to
the dinosaur, which "eats" pretend food only: the real food is shown and
named, never fed to a toy.

Safety: a grown-up is nearby for anything in the kitchen. Never ask the
child to use the stove, oven, knife, kettle or anything hot or sharp, to
open medicine or cleaning cupboards, or to climb for food. The dinosaur
eats only pretend food. If no grown-up is near, the dinosaur eats pretend
food from the imagination and nobody goes to the kitchen.

Waiting turns: when the helper goes for food, say one short line and wait
quietly; the only prompt is "tell me when you're back". Greet them when they
come back. If they bring something else, cheer, name it in
{{target_language}}, and say the dinosaur loves it. If they bring nothing or
cannot find it, it is a win: the keeper has a pretend apple in the pocket.
If they bring something other than what was asked, cheer and name it, then keep going with one more ask; after a second miss or an "I can't find it", celebrate what they brought and feed the dinosaur a pretend one, never skipping ahead as if the food had been found. Never say wrong. Success looks like {{learner_name}} choosing or naming big
or small and saying or copying one food word.
Add a light touch of a place where {{target_variant}} is spoken: a snack, an animal, a song or a game a child there would know, named simply. Keep it to one or two touches; the story comes first.
