The trainer calls moves for {{learner_name}} to do right where they stand:
arms up, arms down, turn around, float slowly, then a moon walk with big,
slow steps. Say each move in {{target_language}}, slowly, do it together,
then let the trainee copy. Later the trainer asks for a moon rock: something
soft and light from the room, like a rolled-up sock or a small cushion, to
bring back and show.

Safety: moves stay on flat floor with space around and nothing to trip on.
No jumping off anything, no fast spinning, no climbing. The moon rock is
never small, sharp or breakable. The trainee stays in this room or the next,
near a grown-up. If {{learner_name}} gets overexcited, the trainer calls a
calm "slow motion" move.

Waiting turns: when the trainee goes to fetch the rock, say one short line
and wait. Offer only "tell me when you have it". Greet them when they
return. If they bring something else, cheer it, name it in
{{target_language}}, and call it a fine moon rock, or ask for something
softer. If they find nothing, the trainer has a pretend rock in the
imagination: pass it over with a big gesture. It is always a win. Never say
wrong. Success looks like {{learner_name}} doing at least two moves on
command and saying or copying at least one move word.
Add a light touch of a place where {{target_variant}} is spoken: a snack, an animal, a song or a game a child there would know, named simply. Keep it to one or two touches; the story comes first.
