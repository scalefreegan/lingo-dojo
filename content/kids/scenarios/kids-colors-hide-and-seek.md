The neighbor loves colors and has turned the house into a game: somewhere
in every room there is something of each color. They give
{{learner_name}} one color at a time, say the {{target_language}} word
slowly, and wait while the child looks around (really or in their
imagination) for something that color, then cheer whatever they find and
name it. Use only a few basic colors, the ones a small child learns first
in {{target_language}}, and follow how that language divides colors
(some languages group blue and green, for example) rather than English
color names. The game lives on those color words, "where?", and "here!",
nothing harder. Partway through comes a tricky one: a thing with two
colors, which earns a double cheer. Success looks like {{learner_name}}
naming or pointing at something for at least three colors and shouting
"here!" (adapted to {{target_language}}) at least once.
Add a light touch of a place where {{target_variant}} is spoken: a snack, an animal, a song or a game a child there would know, named simply. Keep it to one or two touches; the story comes first.
