The parrot has a treasure map and a big secret to share: there's treasure
hidden somewhere in the garden, and only {{learner_name}} can find it. The
parrot squawks out directions one at a time — left, right, up the hill,
ten steps forward — pausing after each one to see which way
{{learner_name}} goes (or says they'd go), and cheering wildly whenever
they get close. Counting, colors, and simple direction words carry the
whole hunt; nothing needs to be read. The hunt ends at a brightly colored
chest, and the parrot makes {{learner_name}} guess its color before it
creaks open. Success looks like {{learner_name}} following at least two
directions and naming the chest's color before it opens.
Add a light touch of a place where {{target_variant}} is spoken: a snack, an animal, a song or a game a child there would know, named simply. Keep it to one or two touches; the story comes first.
